## Per-paper detail

### 1. "A hybrid approach to formulaic alpha discovery with LLM assistance" (HARLA)

Real paper, Frontiers of Computer Science 20(2):2002316, received 2024-09-30, accepted 2025-03-17, published online 2025-10-17. Authors are the same ICT-CAS/AlphaGen lineage (Yu, Xue, Ao, He) behind the KDD-2023 AlphaGen RL alpha-mining paper and GitHub repo (`ICT-FinD-Lab/alphagen`). Read the full open-access PDF (`journal.hep.com.cn` mirror; Springer's own page is login-gated).

- Market/universe: **China A-shares, CSI300 subset only.** No US data anywhere in the paper.
- Sample: train 01/01/2012-12/31/2021, test 01/01/2022-06/30/2023.
- Construction: pure cross-sectional IC/Rank-IC evaluation of formula-generated alpha signals (Table 1), not a disclosed long-only or long-short tradable portfolio with position sizing; Fig. 2 shows "cumulative excess returns" of a simulated backtest but no transaction-cost assumption is stated anywhere in the paper (searched for "cost," "bps," "transaction" -- the only cost discussion is LLM API/compute cost, not trading cost).
- Headline: HARLA IC 0.0515 / ICIR 0.3612 vs baseline AlphaGen IC 0.0288 / ICIR 0.1957 (Table 1) -- a 78.8% IC improvement, close enough to the "75%" and "0.05" the owner quoted.
- Code: no code-availability statement found in the paper; the baseline AlphaGen framework it builds on is open (GitHub), the hybrid HARLA extension itself is not confirmed released.
- Independent replication: none found (too new, Oct 2025). This is one more instance of the LLM-formulaic-alpha-mining cluster our registry already has two entries for (`dir:llm_factor_mining_unverified_claims`: QuantaAlpha non-replicated, Chain-of-Alpha uncoded; `dir:llm_formulaic_alpha_mining_price_volume`: AlphaForge/AlphaGen/alpha-gfn/Alpha-R1, all China-market/GPU-bound). This specific paper is not literally in either list but is the same mechanism, same authors' lineage, same China-only/no-US-validation gap, same missing-cost-disclosure gap.

### 2. "ChatGPT in Systematic Investing -- Enhancing Risk-Adjusted Returns with LLMs"

Real paper, arXiv:2510.26228, submitted 2025-10-30, also an SSI/Swiss Finance Institute working paper (SSRN 5680782). Full PDF read directly.

- Market/universe: S&P 500 constituents, daily returns; news from Stock News API (minute-level timestamps, CNBC/Zacks/Bloomberg/Motley Fool/Fox Business/The Street).
- Sample: 1,382 daily observations total, split into a **validation set Oct 2019-Dec 2023** (1,070 obs, used only for hyperparameter selection) and a **true out-of-sample test set Jan 2024-Mar 2025** (312 obs) -- this window starts after ChatGPT-4o-mini's Oct 2023 pretraining cutoff, so the OOS period is a genuine "model has never seen this" test, not just a calendar holdout.
- Construction: **long-only.** Baseline = classic Jegadeesh-Titman/Carhart cross-sectional momentum, top-decile monthly. LLM extension: broadens the candidate pool to the top-two deciles, scores each stock 0-1 via a news-conditioned prompt ("this stock is about to enter a momentum portfolio -- will momentum continue?"), re-ranks and re-weights, selects top m=50 (optimal hyperparameter from the validation grid).
- Costs: explicit and conservative -- **2bps** per trade, net-of-cost results reported throughout (Table 3). Turnover is higher for the LLM version (0.90 vs 0.62 full-sample) but the paper shows the net-of-cost Sharpe gain survives anyway.
- Headline (Table 3, exact numbers): full sample Sharpe 0.69 (LLM) vs 0.57 (baseline), Sortino 0.69 vs 0.54, return 18% vs 15%, MDD -31% vs -33%. **OOS (Jan 2024-Mar 2025)**: Sharpe 1.06 (LLM) vs 0.79 (baseline), Sortino 1.28 vs 0.93, return 30% vs 24%, MDD -17% vs -19%.
- Caveats the paper itself discloses: the LLM-vs-baseline return-differential regression alpha (3.26%/yr) is significant only at the **10% level**; the 15-month OOS window contains no crisis/drawdown period; monthly rebalancing (not weekly) is required for the effect to survive costs; results are strongest for a concentrated m=50-name portfolio, weaker/noisier for broader coverage.
- Relation to the owner's 2026-09-27 declined leads: distinct mechanism. arXiv 2601.11958 ("Autonomous Market Intelligence: Agentic AI Nowcasting") and MarketSenseAI both use an LLM doing open-ended web search to *pick stocks from scratch*. This paper's LLM only reads pre-supplied, timestamped firm-specific news headlines to *filter/re-weight an already-passing rule-based momentum book* -- structurally the same mechanism as our own already-triaged `dir:llm_acceptance_judge_forward_shadow` (weekly LLM keep/half/skip judge on momentum top-50 using news + insider + 8-K PIT data, DeepSeek endpoint already configured, budget 2, used 0).
- No independent replication found (too new); one critical secondary discussion (an investing newsletter) flagged the same weaknesses the paper discloses itself: 10%-significance alpha, thin 15-month no-crisis OOS window, rising turnover under only 2bps costs.

### 3. "Interacting Anomalies"

Real paper, Review of Asset Pricing Studies 15(2):162-216, published online 2025-01-25 (June 2025 issue). Confirmed via the Oxford Academic abstract page (open, not paywalled).

- Market/universe: US common stocks, CRSP/Compustat/IBES.
- Sample: **1970-2017** primary analysis, **1987-2016** for the machine-learning comparison. **No 2022-2026 coverage whatsoever** -- ends nearly a decade before our target window.
- Construction: all 102 x 101 possible double-sorted anomaly-interaction portfolios (headline is the **long-short** spread); the paper also reports long-only corner portfolios but the Sharpe-2.0 headline the owner quoted is the long-short number.
- Headline (confirmed exact): "equal-weighted (value-weighted) monthly average returns of 4% (2.7%) at an annualized Sharpe ratio of 2 (1.38)" for the out-of-sample best-double-sort strategy -- matches the owner's "Sharpe near 2" precisely.
- Costs: modeled; the paper argues costs would need to be an order of magnitude above Frazzini et al. (2018) estimates to erode the effect.
- Context/counterevidence found in the same search pass: this paper sits inside a literature explicitly flagged for p-hacking risk (Hou-Xue-Zhang's 447-anomaly replication project is cited as the backdrop the authors are trying to correct for with multiple-hypothesis-testing controls).
- Relevance to us: this is the same mechanism as `dir:ml_ranking_liquid500_recent_vs_momentum_rule` -- "combine many published anomalies, rank/screen, beat a simple momentum rule" -- which we already ran directly on our own PIT liquid-500 universe for 2024-2026 and lost (best screened-factor portfolio 17.5-19.4% annualized vs the momentum rule's 33.7%). A 1970-2017 academic long-short result, even a strong one, is not new evidence against that already-run, more-relevant, more-recent local test.

### 4. "Enhancing stock market anomalies with machine learning"

Real paper, published in Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting 60(1):195-230 (2023); the owner's "2022" is the year of the SSRN/working-paper version (SSRN 3752741), which is when this cluster of results first circulated. Full text read via the authors' institutional-repository open-access PDF (RPTU Kaiserslautern).

- Market/universe: US, CRSP (monthly/daily pricing) + Compustat (fundamentals), following Chen-Zimmermann (2020) anomaly construction and lag conventions.
- Sample: anomaly signals **1979-2019**; main training/test split **cross-validation through ~2002, out-of-sample post-2003 through 2019**. **No 2022-2026 coverage.**
- Construction: **long-short** spread portfolios (percent-ranked, long top / short bottom), not long-only.
- Headline (Table 2, exact numbers, value-weighted, out-of-sample column): Baseline (linear factor) Sharpe **0.9229** (in-sample)/**1.0536** (a second baseline reading); GLM **1.2879/1.3868**; GBM **1.0861**. The GLM out-of-sample Sharpe of 1.3868 is the number nearest the owner's "1.3-1.4" claim; the baseline's ~0.92-1.05 matches "≈0.9" closely. Costs: round-trip up to 2% (240bps) disclosed and the paper reports results survive it.
- Independent replication/critique found: this paper's approach is the same family evaluated by Gu-Kelly-Xiu (2020) and later critiqued -- Bryzgalova et al. (2023) found many ML-enhanced anomalies "not out-of-sample replicable," and multiple post-2020 studies document degrading R² and Sharpe once microcaps are excluded and realistic costs/post-2019 data are used.
- Relevance to us: this is literally the hypothesis our own `dir:ml_ranking_liquid500_recent_vs_momentum_rule` and `dir:baseline_chain_b0_b1_b2_market_neutral_vs_long` already tested on our real, current (2018-2026) US-equity data, using a comparable design (screen a public factor library, train ridge/LightGBM rankers, compare to a momentum baseline) -- and it lost on every metric that matters (realized CAGR) in every configuration tried. A 1979-2019 academic long-short result does not overturn a more recent, more relevant, already-completed local test.

### 5. "Multifactor Timing with Deep Learning"

Real paper (exact published title is "Multi-Factor Timing with Deep Learning"), Journal of Financial Econometrics 24(3):nbag006 (2026 issue), Cotturo/Liu/Proner. Confirmed via the Oxford Academic (JFEC) article page; the full paywalled PDF was not independently obtained, so these numbers come from one extraction pass rather than two, flagged as slightly lower-confidence than papers 1/2/4/6.

- Market/universe: the five Fama-French factor portfolios (SMB/HML/RMW/CMA/WML) as the assets being timed -- not individual stocks.
- Sample: **Jan 1965-Dec 2021** (57 years); out-of-sample rolling-window test **Jan 1990-Dec 2021** (32 annual folds, expanding training window). **No 2022-2026 coverage.**
- Construction: long-only/flat timing overlay -- go long a factor when the model's predicted probability exceeds 50%, otherwise hold cash; no short-selling in the main strategy (the underlying factor portfolios are themselves long-short, but the *timing* decision is binary long/flat).
- Headline: DMT (deep model) Sharpe **0.82**, linear-regression baseline **0.61**, buy-and-hold **0.60**; after 14bps transaction costs, DMT Sharpe **0.74** with alpha 1.01%/yr (t=2.21). Matches the owner's "deep ~0.8, above linear" claim closely.
- Relevance to us: this is the same underlying mechanism our registry already flags as decayed -- `dir:factor_momentum_timing_decay` cites Blitz (Robeco, 2026): the same five FF factors' short-and-medium-term timing/momentum earns 2.8%/yr and 1.7%/yr full-sample (1963-2024) but decays to ~0.4%/yr **post-2011**, with the medium-term version 67%-correlated with plain stock-level WML. This DMT paper's own sample stops in 2021 and does not report a post-2011-only or 2022+ split, so it neither confirms nor refutes the specific post-2011 decay Blitz documents -- it is simply silent on exactly the period we care about (recent regime).

### 6. "Automate Strategy Finding with LLM in Quant Investment"

Real paper, Findings of ACL: EMNLP 2025, pp.18517-18533 (also arXiv:2409.06289, first posted Sep 2024, last revised Nov 2025). Full PDF read directly from ACL Anthology (open access). Code is released: `github.com/kouzhizhuo/Automate-Strategy-Finding-with-LLM-in-Quant-investment`, 77 stars / 26 forks, last pushed 2026-08-06 (checked via GitHub API) -- a real, apparently maintained repo, unlike most of the papers in this cluster.

- Market/universe: **primarily Chinese (SSE50, CSI300)**; a secondary set of "US market" results is reported using SP500 constituents over the same short windows.
- Sample: three 6-month train/validate/test windows (e.g. Jan-Jun 2021/2022/2023), both for CSI300 and SP500 (Table 5). Headline 53.17% cumulative return is the **SSE50, Jan 2023-Jan 2024** result specifically.
- Construction: not clearly stated as long-only or long-short; a turnover cap (k=13 factors, n=5 trades/day, ~38%/day turnover) is used "to optimize transaction costs," but **no explicit cost bps or dollar cost model is disclosed anywhere in the paper** -- costs are managed only by capping trade count, not measured.
- Headline numbers, read directly from the paper's own tables, are **not credible as risk-adjusted performance**: Table 6 reports "Jan-Jun 2023" strategy annual returns of 192.27% (CSI300) and 118.24% (SP500) against benchmarks of 9.13% and 42.78% respectively; the sensitivity tables (9-10) report Sharpe ratios of 8.10, 9.00, 9.69, 10.37, 11.39, and 13.33 across different hyperparameter/weight configurations, treated by the authors as evidence of "robustness." For context, sustained institutional live Sharpe ratios above 2-3 are essentially unheard of (Berkshire Hathaway's full-history Sharpe is 0.79); Sharpe ratios in the 8-13 range on 6-month test windows are a strong signature of a very small sample size and/or a methodological error (annualization, look-ahead, or survivorship), not of a working strategy. No independent replication or third-party critique was found (paper is Nov 2025), but this is a self-evident red flag on the paper's own numbers, not a citation-dependent judgment.
- Relevance to us: same general mechanism (LLM proposes -> multi-agent filters/backtests -> optimizes weights -> re-hypothesizes) as our own already-adopted `dir:llm_hypothesis_code_backtest_loop` (the RD-Agent-style loop we already chose as our primary pattern, distinct from the Docker/GPU-bound `dir:rd_agent_q`). The paper's specific contribution worth noting for that loop's design -- a confidence-score + risk-preference gating layer on top of raw IC screening -- is the same idea already covered by folding in CogAlpha's 5-dimension filter (`dir:cogalpha_llm_code_alpha_generation`, already parked/folded). Nothing in this paper's own reported numbers should be used as an expected-performance reference; only the loop *shape* is worth anything here, and we already adopted that shape.
