                                                                                            Abstract

                                                      Can a residual from a fitted return-decay curve predict future factor tail losses, and
                                                 does it measure crowding? We separate economic edge, overlapping performance statistics,
                                                 and predictive evidence. A Gaussian counterexample produces a same-month crash-risk
                                                 ratio of 1.61 without predictable future returns. An eight-factor US reconstruction finds no
                                                 robust advantage for the tested residual under its original specification. We then freeze and
                                                 execute a geographic extension with six factors in Europe, Japan, and Asia Pacific excluding
                                                 Japan, using matched feature baselines, frozen and annually updated forecasts, and separate
                                                 2015–2024 and 2025–August 2026 evaluations. Main-era pooled regional log loss increases
                                                 by 0.00704 with frozen augmentation and 0.00233 with annual refitting. Yet Brier rankings
                                                 differ, and a matched-history US comparison favors the residual. A controlled diagnostic
                                                 reproduces the US ranking reversals. A further mechanism study holds observations and
                                                 labels fixed to isolate the age-dependent candidate family: its effect is secondary in the
                                                 pooled reversal. Holding A’s penalties fixed largely removes the reversal, but reversing the
                                                 decomposition anchor changes the allocation between history and reselection. The largest
                                                 changes concentrate in a factor with zero or one validation crash. Complete penalty paths
                                                 further distinguish changes across feature histories from sensitivity to individual validation
                                                 months: the zero-event case is stable under all such deletions. Established sequential forecast
                                                 bounds permit dependent, nonstationary comparisons under an explicitly disclosed research
                                                 filtration; all 48 intervals include zero and permit nontrivial benefits. An equivalent-basis
                                                 experiment further isolates regularization effects, with large basis gaps often reflecting poor
                                                 component forecasts rather than substantial gains over a simple Sharpe baseline. The findings
                                                 establish sensitivity of the tested evaluation to representation, target definition, and scoring
                                                 rule, not universal proxy failure, crowding identification, or prospective forecasting value. All
                                                 forecasts, unfavorable findings, configurations, and provenance are retained.



                                            1    Introduction

                                            Factor returns can change after a strategy becomes widely known, but return decay does not
                                            uniquely identify investor crowding. Changes in risk premia, volatility, implementation costs,
                                            sample composition, and chance can generate similar patterns. Evidence on post-publication
                                            predictability [2] motivates studying competition, while also making it necessary to separate
                                            economic mechanisms from descriptive curve fitting.
                                            The earlier manuscript [1] linked a hyperbolic curve to a trading game, fitted the curve to rolling
                                            factor Sharpe ratios, and interpreted prediction residuals as crowding. It reported a weak timing
                                            strategy and heterogeneous tail-risk associations. This revision retains those research questions
                                            while correcting three inferential steps: the equilibrium calculation, the mapping from economic
                                            edge to a Sharpe statistic, and the interpretation of contemporaneous residuals as forecasts.

The central question is: how stable is the incremental forecasting value of a decay residual
across markets, target definitions, and matched representations of the same past returns? A gain
against a restricted learner can support a representation choice without identifying crowding.
Conversely, an unfavorable score under one reconstruction cannot establish universal failure. The
external results and setup diagnostics below make both distinctions consequential. A mechanistic
follow-up tests and weakens an initially plausible explanation: resetting the hyperbolic age origin
changes the admissible curves, but history and penalty reselection interact in the observed pooled
reversal, with the allocation depending on the decomposition anchor.


Contribution and scope. This revision separates economic edge, descriptive decay, and
predictive evidence; gives a timing counterexample tailored to the proposed residual; and
evaluates matched representations in the US and three external geographic universes. It applies
established sequential forecast-comparison theory to report what the regional evidence can resolve
under dependence and drift. The Cournot-style calculation, conditional-expectation identity,
proper scoring rules, and block-resampling principles are established tools. We do not claim a
new general learning algorithm or a new general information-theoretic theorem. A controlled
computational study separates changed fitting observations, changed candidate families and
penalty reselection, retaining the disconfirmation of a dominant origin effect. The reconstruction
evaluates one transparent implementation of the question; it is not an exact replication or a test
of every possible crowding proxy.


Related evidence. Post-publication return declines studied by McLean and Pontiff [2] motivate
a competition hypothesis without uniquely identifying it. Falck, Rej, and Thesmar [7] also analyze
performance deterioration in relation to overfitting and arbitrage hypotheses. DeMiguel, Martín-
Utrera, and Uppal [8] study how trading across characteristics can alleviate price-impact costs;
our single-signal model excludes that mechanism. Barroso, Edelen, and Karehnke [9] explicitly
distinguish feedback assumptions and examine institutional-holdings proxies, finding evidence
inconsistent with crowding as an unconditional source of momentum tail risk. A return-curve
residual is not the holdings measure in that work. These distinctions preclude treating a similar
sign in a residual-based table as a replication of their mechanism.


2    Measurement and data

The economic motivation is inverse-linear residual edge e(t) = a/(N (t) + 1) under a stylized
quantity game, not inverse-linear trader profit. Appendix A gives the corrected equilibrium,
Poisson-entry calculation, and restrictive bridge to a population Sharpe. It also proves that
changing opportunities can reproduce the same edge path without changing participation, and
that equal Sharpe ratios need not imply equal tail probabilities. These restrictions motivate
treating the fitted residual as a predictive representation, not a participation estimate.
The historical study uses monthly US factor returns from the Kenneth French Data Library [3]:
market, size, value, profitability, investment, momentum, and short- and long-term reversal. A
replication must archive the exact downloaded series, date range, definitions, units, and data
vintage. In particular, the SMB construction must be consistent across merged factor files. The
original narrative ends the sample in 2024 while its strategy table is labeled 2017–2025; the input
manifest must resolve this discrepancy.

The first statement is a general measurability fact, not a new information-theoretic result. Its
value here is to define the paper’s empirical interpretation precisely. It does not imply equal
finite-sample performance for restricted learners. A nonlinear ratio or a historically fitted trend
may be a useful feature for a simple model. Regularization can also change when redundant
columns are added, so penalized results require an explicit tuning comparison rather than an
appeal to the unpenalized equivalence.
The appropriate hierarchy is: (i) simple trailing-return and volatility controls; (ii) a component-
matched model with the curve prediction and estimated Sharpe separately, plus flexible transfor-
mations chosen on development data; (iii) the residual representation with comparable tuning;
and (iv) independent holdings or flow inputs when available. An improvement at (iii) supports a
useful representation under the tested learning procedure. Evidence for a crowding mechanism
additionally requires independent measurement or an identification design. Merely adding Ct
to a linear model in mean and volatility does not separate an economic mechanism from the
nonlinear mean/volatility ratio.


4     Retrospective public-data reconstruction

4.1    Data, estimands, and chronology

We downloaded the official monthly five-factor, momentum, short-term reversal, and long-term
reversal archives on 26 September 2026. All four headers identify the August 2026 CRSP database.
The analysis uses July 1963–December 2024, a complete 738-month calendar for all eight factors.
Mkt-RF, SMB, HML, RMW, and CMA are taken from one five-factor file to avoid mixing SMB
definitions. Values are divided by 100 once; RF is not subtracted again from Mkt-RF or from
long-short spreads. SHA-256 hashes, source URLs, and retrieval timestamps are frozen in the
repository. Data after December 2024 are not used in this original eight-factor reconstruction;
the separately specified extension below uses observations through August 2026.
This is a new-vintage reconstruction. It does not reproduce the original positive-only 1995–2024
fit, original threshold-training window, undocumented preprocessing, or strategy. The evaluation

4.2    Curve estimation and prediction models

The primary window is 36 months. At each origin t, fit the hyperbola to every finite signed
Sharpe statistic ending strictly before t, requiring at least 60 such observations. Set age zero
at the first complete window and measure age in years. Search λ ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3}
per year; for each value, estimate nonnegative amplitude by least squares. The minimizing
past-sample squared error determines m b t|t−1 . Zero decay is allowed, so a fitted constant is a valid
outcome, not silently discarded. A sensitivity allows signed amplitude. Constant, exponential,
linear, and one-step persistence forecasts of the rolling statistic are compared on the same dates.
Separate ridge logistic models are fit for each factor. The control model contains the latest
available return, trailing mean, trailing standard deviation, and calendar age. The augmented
model adds Ct . The component model adds m      b t|t−1 and Sbt separately. Flexible controls include
the Sharpe ratio, squares of return/mean/volatility/Sharpe, and a mean–volatility interaction in
addition to the controls. A historical-event-rate predictor uses (n1 + 1/2)/(n + 1) on development
labels. No class reweighting or outcome-based test-period selection is used.
For each nonconstant model, standardize features using training values and minimize mean
log loss plus α∥β∥22 /2, with an unpenalized intercept. Select α ∈ {0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} by
validation log loss separately for each factor/model, then refit through 2014 with newly fitted
development scalers. Components and residuals have different regularization geometries and
dimensions even though they share generating information. Their comparison evaluates the
specified learning procedures, not unrestricted conditional information content. All tuning losses
and fitted coefficients are retained.


4.3    Dependence and multiplicity

We resample aligned monthly vectors of paired loss differences using circular blocks, preserving
both temporal runs and contemporaneous cross-factor dependence. The primary block length
is 12 months, with 6, 24, and 36 as sensitivity values, and 4,000 resamples with seed 20260926.
This follows the established block-bootstrap rationale for dependent observations [6]; it is not an

iid bootstrap of factor-month rows. The macro resampling range is percentile-based. For the
eight primary factor contrasts, joint ranges use the 95th percentile of the maximum absolute
centered bootstrap mean divided by each factor’s bootstrap standard error. This family does not
include all sensitivity/model comparisons, which remain exploratory.
The realized ∆b is exactly known for the retained forecasts and outcomes. We therefore report
the bands as descriptive resampling ranges, not established 95% confidence intervals for that
finite sample or a future era. Under suitable stationarity and weak-dependence conditions, a
conditional population target could be ∆ = E[ℓ(Y, paug ) − ℓ(Y, pctrl ) | development information].
Those conditions are not established for this recursively constructed, drifting series. Models,
thresholds, and hyperparameters are not refitted inside this loss bootstrap. The nominal
resampling quantiles consequently do not establish coverage, learning-procedure uncertainty, or
absence of a practically useful effect.

Calendar concentration and forecast drift. A further post-results diagnostic holds every
primary forecast fixed and reports all ten calendar years and both five-year halves (Appendix D.1).
Omitting any single year’s evaluation rows leaves a positive mean loss difference, ranging from
0.00179 to 0.00270. Thus no single omitted year reverses the observed average ranking. However,
about 64% of the summed deterioration occurs in 2023–2024, and augmentation improves the
annual score in 2016, 2020, and 2021. The result is heterogeneous rather than a uniform penalty
in every period.
Across 2015–2019 and 2020–2024, the observed event frequency rises from 66/480 (13.75%) to
97/480 (20.21%), whereas the augmented model’s mean probability changes from approximately
10.96% to 11.79%. These marginal discrepancies reinforce the limits on operational calibration
and stationary-bootstrap interpretation. The calendar analysis is descriptive: removing evaluation
rows does not refit models or remove those observations’ influence on later rolling features. It
supplies neither independent confirmation nor a new significance test.

8    Limitations and conclusion

The corrected game supplies a restrictive economic motivation, while the Gaussian null demon-
strates a mechanical same-month association. Neither identifies crowding. A residual computed
from past returns concerns representation conditional on its generating history. Shared shocks,
overlapping windows, limited independent temporal information and revised data vintages
constrain inference and historical replication.
Forecast rankings vary across markets, target definitions and scores. The controlled US follow-up
shows that age-dependent candidate families have a small pooled effect, while fitting history and
penalty reselection interact, with large changes concentrated in CMA. The allocation between
history and reselection depends on the decomposition anchor; CMA has zero or one validation
event. A second diagnostic separates history sensitivity from deletion sensitivity: the zero-event
case is stable under every single-month deletion. Exact family transport preserves a chosen
model class; no general repair or forecasting improvement is established. These findings explain
a specific procedure comparison and retain counterexamples to broader claims.
The equivalent-basis study confirms that native regularization can materially change forecasts
without changing the linear function class. Exact penalty transport restores equality but does
not improve the reference estimator; useful superiority over strong simple baselines remains
unestablished.

B.4    Independent crowding evidence and trading relevance

ETF activity can reflect many mechanisms. Correlating cumulative residuals with a trending
volume series is vulnerable to common trends. A mechanistic extension should specify the
ETF universe, constituent mapping, source, volume or asset measure, transformations, and lag
convention; then test incremental associations after trend and market controls. Holdings overlap,
flows, and strategy capacity are potential independent measurements, not interchangeable proxies.
For trading, derive positions from information available at the execution time, use identical risk
targets across benchmarks, and report turnover, costs, financing, gross and net performance, and
uncertainty. A failed strategy cannot prove market efficiency or exclude useful alternative signals.
Conversely, a statistical tail association does not by itself justify position limits or stop-loss rules;
those decisions require a specified loss function and an evaluation of the resulting policy.

Reproducibility and evidence boundaries. The repository contains immutable original
PDF, frozen public input archives, a hash-checked parser, configuration, selected penalties and
coefficients, generated tables, dated prediction ledgers, and validation scripts. Commands are in
the README. The core return analysis and external ETF activity analysis have been rebuilt
from public observations. Exact historical replication remains a separate provenance question
requiring original files; it is not a prerequisite for executing these new studies. Mechanism
identification and certified historical availability still require independent measurements and
release-time records. The eight reconstructed factor rows do not resolve omissions from the

Cross-repository source recovery. We inspected the author’s ai-in-finance repository,
including four available branches and 743 reachable commits. A December 2025 research note
explicitly describes this paper’s hyperbolic model and factor taxonomy, but the searched history
does not supply its eight-factor implementation or archived forecast outputs. The similarly named
causal-factor project concerns a different daily regime study. Its available 25-portfolio daily-return
cache and return-loading-based co-exposure scores do not constitute the original monthly inputs
or independent holdings observations. An IWM-volume download script likewise targets the
other study’s Granger statistics. The source-recovery audit records commit identifiers, artifact
hashes, and remaining requirements; no result from that project is imported as a replication or
crowding validation here.
