<dd><p>Mean-Risk Optimization estimator.</p>
<p>The below 4 objective functions can be optimized:</p>
<blockquote>
<div><ul class="simple">
<li><p>Minimize Risk:</p></li>
</ul>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[\begin{split}\begin{cases}
\begin{aligned}
&amp;\min_{w} &amp; &amp; risk_{i}(w) \\
&amp;\text{s.t.} &amp; &amp; w^T \cdot \mu \ge min\_return \\
&amp; &amp; &amp; A \cdot w \ge b \\
&amp; &amp; &amp; risk_{j}(w) \le max\_risk_{j} \quad \forall \; j \ne i
\end{aligned}
\end{cases}\end{split}\]</div>
<ul class="simple">
<li><p>Maximize Expected Return:</p></li>
</ul>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[\begin{split}\begin{cases}
\begin{aligned}
&amp;\max_{w} &amp; &amp; w^T \cdot \mu \\
&amp;\text{s.t.} &amp; &amp; risk_{i}(w) \le max\_risk_{i} \\
&amp; &amp; &amp; A \cdot w \ge b \\
&amp; &amp; &amp; risk_{j}(w) \le max\_risk_{j} \quad \forall \; j \ne i
\end{aligned}
\end{cases}\end{split}\]</div>
<ul class="simple">
<li><p>Maximize Utility:</p></li>
</ul>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[\begin{split}\begin{cases}
\begin{aligned}
&amp;\max_{w} &amp; &amp; w^T \cdot \mu - \lambda \times risk_{i}(w)\\
&amp;\text{s.t.} &amp; &amp; risk_{i}(w) \le max\_risk_{i} \\
&amp; &amp; &amp; w^T \cdot \mu \ge min\_return \\
&amp; &amp; &amp; A \cdot w \ge b \\
&amp; &amp; &amp; risk_{j}(w) \le max\_risk_{j} \quad \forall \; j \ne i
\end{aligned}
\end{cases}\end{split}\]</div>
<ul class="simple">
<li><p>Maximize Ratio:</p></li>
</ul>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[\begin{split}\begin{cases}
\begin{aligned}
&amp;\max_{w} &amp; &amp; \frac{w^T \cdot \mu - r_{f}}{risk_{i}(w)}\\
&amp;\text{s.t.} &amp; &amp; risk_{i}(w) \le max\_risk_{i} \\
&amp; &amp; &amp; w^T \cdot \mu \ge min\_return \\
&amp; &amp; &amp; A \cdot w \ge b \\
&amp; &amp; &amp; risk_{j}(w) \le max\_risk_{j} \quad \forall \; j \ne i
\end{aligned}
\end{cases}\end{split}\]</div>
</div></blockquote>
<p>With <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(risk_{i}\)</span> a risk measure among:</p>
<blockquote>
<div><ul class="simple">
<li><p>Mean Absolute Deviation</p></li>
<li><p>First Lower Partial Moment</p></li>
<li><p>Variance</p></li>
<li><p>Semi-Variance</p></li>
<li><p>CVaR (Conditional Value at Risk)</p></li>
<li><p>EVaR (Entropic Value at Risk)</p></li>
<li><p>Worst Realization (worst return)</p></li>
<li><p>CDaR (Conditional Drawdown at Risk)</p></li>
<li><p>Maximum Drawdown</p></li>
<li><p>Average Drawdown</p></li>
<li><p>EDaR (Entropic Drawdown at Risk)</p></li>
<li><p>Ulcer Index</p></li>
<li><p>Gini Mean Difference</p></li>
</ul>
</div></blockquote>
<p>Cost, regularization, uncertainty set, and additional constraints can also be added
to the optimization problem (see the parameters description).</p>
<p>The expected asset returns, covariance matrix and returns are estimated from the
<a class="reference internal" href="../user_guide/prior.html#prior"><span class="std std-ref">prior estimator</span></a>.</p>

The default value is <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">1.0</span></code> (fully invested portfolio).</p>
<p>For example:</p>
<blockquote>
<div><ul class="simple">
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">budget</span> <span class="pre">=</span> <span class="pre">1</span></code> –&gt; fully invested portfolio.</p></li>
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">budget</span> <span class="pre">=</span> <span class="pre">0</span></code> –&gt; market neutral portfolio.</p></li>
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">budget</span> <span class="pre">=</span> <span class="pre">None</span></code> –&gt; no constraints on the sum of weights.</p></li>
</ul>
</div></blockquote>
</dd>
<dt><strong>min_budget</strong><span class="classifier">float, optional</span></dt><dd><p>Minimum budget. It is the lower bound of the sum of long and short positions
(sum of all weights). If provided, you must set <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">budget=None</span></code>.
The default (<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">None</span></code>) means no minimum budget constraint.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>max_budget</strong><span class="classifier">float, optional</span></dt><dd><p>Maximum budget. It is the upper bound of the sum of long and short positions
(sum of all weights). If provided, you must set <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">budget=None</span></code>.
The default (<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">None</span></code>) means no maximum budget constraint.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>max_short</strong><span class="classifier">float, optional</span></dt><dd><p>Maximum short position. The short position is defined as the sum of negative
weights (in absolute term).
The default (<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">None</span></code>) means no maximum short position.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>max_long</strong><span class="classifier">float, optional</span></dt><dd><p>Maximum long position. The long position is defined as the sum of positive
weights.
The default (<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">None</span></code>) means no maximum long position.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>cardinality</strong><span class="classifier">int, optional</span></dt><dd><p>Specifies the cardinality constraint to limit the number of invested assets
(non-zero weights). This feature requires a mixed-integer solver. For an
open-source option, we recommend using SCIP by setting <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">solver=&quot;SCIP&quot;</span></code>.
To install it, use: <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">pip</span> <span class="pre">install</span> <span class="pre">cvxpy[SCIP]</span></code>. For commercial solvers,
supported options include MOSEK, GUROBI, or CPLEX.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>group_cardinalities</strong><span class="classifier">dict[str, int], optional</span></dt><dd><p>A dictionary specifying cardinality constraints for specific groups of assets.
The keys represent group names (strings), and the values specify the maximum
number of assets allowed in each group. You must provide the groups using the
<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">groups</span></code> parameter. This requires a mixed-integer solver (see <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">cardinality</span></code>
for more details).</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>threshold_long</strong><span class="classifier">float | dict[str, float] | array-like of shape (n_assets, ), optional</span></dt><dd><p>Specifies the minimum weight threshold for assets in the portfolio to be
considered as a long position. Assets with weights below this threshold
will not be included as part of the portfolio’s long positions. This
constraint can help eliminate insignificant allocations.
This requires a mixed-integer solver (see <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">cardinality</span></code> for more details).
It follows the same format as <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">min_weights</span></code> and <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">max_weights</span></code>.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>threshold_short</strong><span class="classifier">float | dict[str, float] | array-like of shape (n_assets, ), optional</span></dt><dd><p>Specifies the maximum weight threshold for assets in the portfolio to be
considered as a short position. Assets with weights above this threshold
will not be included as part of the portfolio’s short positions. This
constraint can help control the magnitude of short positions.
This requires a mixed-integer solver (see <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">cardinality</span></code> for more details).
It follows the same format as <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">min_weights</span></code> and <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">max_weights</span></code>.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>transaction_costs</strong><span class="classifier">float | dict[str, float] | array-like of shape (n_assets, ), default=0.0</span></dt><dd><p>Transaction costs of the assets. It is used to add linear transaction costs to
the optimization problem:</p>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[total\_cost = \sum_{i=1}^{N} c_{i} \times |w_{i} - w\_prev_{i}|\]</div>
<p>with <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(c_{i}\)</span> the transaction cost of asset i, <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(w_{i}\)</span> its weight
and <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(w\_prev_{i}\)</span> its previous weight (defined in <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">previous_weights</span></code>).
The float <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(total\_cost\)</span> is impacting the portfolio expected return in the optimization:</p>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[expected\_return = \mu^{T} \cdot w - total\_cost\]</div>
<p>with <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(\mu\)</span> the vector of assets’ expected returns and <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(w\)</span> the
vector of assets weights.</p>
<p>For positions in <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">previous_weights</span></code> whose assets are no longer in the
investment universe, transaction costs are calculated assuming full
liquidation. These costs are included in both the optimization and
<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">Portfolio.total_cost</span></code>. For assets absent from <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">X</span></code>, <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">transaction_costs</span></code>
must be a single rate applied to all assets or a dictionary keyed by asset name.</p>
<p>If a float is provided, it is applied to each asset.
If a dictionary is provided, its (key/value) pair must be the
(asset name/asset cost) and the input <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">X</span></code> of the <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">fit</span></code> method must be a
DataFrame with the assets names in columns.
The default value is <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">0.0</span></code>.</p>
<div class="admonition warning">
<p class="admonition-title">Warning</p>
<p>Based on the above formula, the periodicity of the transaction costs
must match the periodicity of <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(\mu\)</span>. For example, if the input
<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">X</span></code> is composed of <strong>daily</strong> returns, the <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">transaction_costs</span></code> need to be
expressed as <strong>daily</strong> costs. A transaction cost is paid once per
rebalancing while a position earns its expected return on every period it
is held, so the one-off cost is converted by dividing it by the expected
investment duration (e.g. <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">0.001</span> <span class="pre">/</span> <span class="pre">21</span></code> for a 10 bps cost with daily
returns and a one-month expected holding period).
(See <a class="reference internal" href="../user_guide/data_preparation.html#periodicity-convention"><span class="std std-ref">Periodicity Convention</span></a>)</p>
</div>
</dd>
<dt><strong>management_fees</strong><span class="classifier">float | dict[str, float] | array-like of shape (n_assets, ), default=0.0</span></dt><dd><p>Management fees of the assets. It is used to add linear management fees to the
optimization problem:</p>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[total\_fee = \sum_{i=1}^{N} f_{i} \times w_{i}\]</div>
<p>with <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(f_{i}\)</span> the management fee of asset i and <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(w_{i}\)</span> its weight.
The float <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(total\_fee\)</span> is impacting the portfolio expected return in the optimization:</p>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[expected\_return = \mu^{T} \cdot w - total\_fee\]</div>
<p>with <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(\mu\)</span> the vector of assets’ expected returns and <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(w\)</span> the vector
of assets weights.</p>
<p>If a float is provided, it is applied to each asset.
If a dictionary is provided, its (key/value) pair must be the
(asset name/asset fee) and the input <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">X</span></code> of the <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">fit</span></code> method must be a
DataFrame with the assets names in columns.
The default value is <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">0.0</span></code>.</p>
<div class="admonition warning">
<p class="admonition-title">Warning</p>
<p>Based on the above formula, the periodicity of the management fees
must match the periodicity of <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(\mu\)</span>. For example, if the input
<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">X</span></code> is composed of <strong>daily</strong> returns, the <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">management_fees</span></code> need to be
expressed in <strong>daily</strong> fees. Unlike transaction costs, management fees
accrue with holding time, so a stated annual fee converts directly to the
return periodicity (e.g. <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">0.02</span> <span class="pre">/</span> <span class="pre">252</span></code> for a 2% annual fee on daily
returns).</p>
</div>
<div class="admonition note">
<p class="admonition-title">Note</p>
<p>Another approach is to directly impact the management fees to the input <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">X</span></code>
in order to express the returns net of fees. However, when estimating the
<span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(\mu\)</span> parameter using for example Shrinkage estimators, this approach
would mix a deterministic value with an uncertain one leading to unwanted
bias in the management fees.</p>
</div>
</dd>
<dt><strong>previous_weights</strong><span class="classifier">float | dict[str, float] | array-like of shape (n_assets, ), optional</span></dt><dd><p>Previous weights of the assets. Previous weights are used to compute the
portfolio cost and the portfolio turnover.
For named positions in assets absent from <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">X</span></code>, these calculations assume
full liquidation.
If a float is provided, it is applied to each asset.
If a dictionary is provided, its (key/value) pair must be the
(asset name/asset previous weight) and the input <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">X</span></code> of the <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">fit</span></code> method must
be a DataFrame with the assets names in columns.
The default (<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">None</span></code>) means no previous weights.
Additionally, when <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">fallback=&quot;previous_weights&quot;</span></code>, failures will fall back to
these weights if provided.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>target_weights</strong><span class="classifier">float | dict[str, float] | array-like of shape (n_assets, ), optional</span></dt><dd><p>Target weights of the assets. When provided, risk measures are computed on the
deviation from these target weights (w - target_weights) instead of the
absolute weights. This is useful for tracking error minimization or when
optimizing around a specific allocation.
If a float is provided, it is applied to each asset.
If a dictionary is provided, its (key/value) pair must be the
(asset name/asset target weight) and the input <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">X</span></code> of the <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">fit</span></code> method must
be a DataFrame with the assets names in columns.
When using a dictionary, assets values that are not provided are assigned
a target weight of <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">0.0</span></code>.
The default (<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">None</span></code>) means no target weights.</p>
<div class="admonition seealso">
<p class="admonition-title">See also</p>
<p><a class="reference internal" href="../user_guide/optimization.html#tracking-error-optimization"><span class="std std-ref">Tracking Error Optimization</span></a></p>
</div>
</dd>
<dt><strong>l1_coef</strong><span class="classifier">float, default=0.0</span></dt><dd><p>L1 regularization coefficient.
It is used to penalize the objective function by the L1 norm:</p>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[l1\_coef \times \Vert w \Vert_{1} = l1\_coef \times \sum_{i=1}^{N} |w_{i}|\]</div>
<p>Increasing this coefficient will reduce the number of non-zero weights
(cardinality). It tends to increase robustness (out-of-sample stability) but
reduces diversification.
The default value is <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">0.0</span></code>.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>l2_coef</strong><span class="classifier">float, default=0.0</span></dt><dd><p>L2 regularization coefficient.
It is used to penalize the objective function by the L2 norm:</p>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[l2\_coef \times \Vert w \Vert_{2}^{2} = l2\_coef \times \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_{i}^2\]</div>
<p>It tends to increase robustness (out-of-sample stability).
The default value is <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">0.0</span></code>.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>mu_uncertainty_set_estimator</strong><span class="classifier">BaseMuUncertaintySet, optional</span></dt><dd><p><a class="reference internal" href="../user_guide/uncertainty_set.html#uncertainty-set-estimator"><span class="std std-ref">Mu Uncertainty set estimator</span></a>.
If provided, the expected asset returns are modelled with a norm-ball
uncertainty set. It is called worst-case optimization and is a class of robust
optimization. It reduces the instability that arises from the estimation errors
of the expected returns.
The worst-case portfolio expected return is:</p>
<div class="math notranslate nohighlight">
\[w^T \cdot \hat{\mu} - \kappa_{\mu} \lVert L_{\mu}^T \cdot w \rVert_{q}\]</div>
<p>with <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(\kappa\)</span> the radius of the uncertainty set (confidence region),
<span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(L\)</span> its linear geometry map and <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(q\)</span> the dual norm. For an
ellipsoidal set with shape matrix <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(S\)</span>, <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(L\)</span> is a square-root factor
satisfying <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(S = L L^T\)</span> and <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(q\)</span> is <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(2\)</span>.
The default (<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">None</span></code>) means that no uncertainty set is used.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>covariance_uncertainty_set_estimator</strong><span class="classifier">BaseCovarianceUncertaintySet, optional</span></dt><dd><p><a class="reference internal" href="../user_guide/uncertainty_set.html#uncertainty-set-estimator"><span class="std std-ref">Covariance Uncertainty set estimator</span></a>.
If provided, covariance estimation uncertainty is included in the optimized
variance. This approach is known as worst-case optimization, a form of robust
optimization. It reduces sensitivity to covariance estimation errors.
Covariance uncertainty is applied when <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">risk_measure=RiskMeasure.VARIANCE</span></code> or
when <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">max_variance</span></code> is set.
The default (<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">None</span></code>) means that no uncertainty set is used.</p>
</dd>
<dt><strong>linear_constraints</strong><span class="classifier">array-like of shape (n_constraints,), optional</span></dt><dd><p>Linear constraints on portfolio weights or factor exposures.</p>
<p>Constraint names can reference:</p>
<blockquote>
<div><ul class="simple">
<li><p>Asset names: individual asset weights (e.g. <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;SPX&quot;</span></code>, <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;AAPL&quot;</span></code>)</p></li>
<li><p>Group names: sums of weights in groups defined by <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">groups</span></code></p></li>
<li><p>Factor names: portfolio factor exposure (requires factor model prior)</p></li>
<li><p>Factor families: sum of portfolio exposures to all factors in one family</p></li>
</ul>
</div></blockquote>
<p>Supported equation patterns include:</p>
<blockquote>
<div><ul class="simple">
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;name</span> <span class="pre">&lt;=</span> <span class="pre">value&quot;</span></code> or <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;name</span> <span class="pre">&gt;=</span> <span class="pre">value&quot;</span></code></p></li>
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;name</span> <span class="pre">==</span> <span class="pre">value&quot;</span></code></p></li>
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;a</span> <span class="pre">*</span> <span class="pre">name1</span> <span class="pre">+</span> <span class="pre">b</span> <span class="pre">*</span> <span class="pre">name2</span> <span class="pre">&lt;=</span> <span class="pre">c</span> <span class="pre">*</span> <span class="pre">name3</span> <span class="pre">+</span> <span class="pre">d&quot;</span></code></p></li>
</ul>
</div></blockquote>
<p>For example:</p>
<blockquote>
<div><ul class="simple">
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;SPX</span> <span class="pre">&gt;=</span> <span class="pre">0.10&quot;</span></code> –&gt; SPX weight &gt;= 10%</p></li>
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;SX5E</span> <span class="pre">+</span> <span class="pre">SPX</span> <span class="pre">&gt;=</span> <span class="pre">0.2&quot;</span></code> –&gt; sum of SX5E and SPX weights &gt;= 20%</p></li>
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;US</span> <span class="pre">==</span> <span class="pre">0.7&quot;</span></code> –&gt; sum of weights in US group == 70%</p></li>
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;Equity</span> <span class="pre">==</span> <span class="pre">3</span> <span class="pre">*</span> <span class="pre">Bond&quot;</span></code> –&gt; sum of weights in Equity group == 3x sum of weights in Bond group</p></li>
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;Momentum</span> <span class="pre">&lt;=</span> <span class="pre">0.30&quot;</span></code> –&gt; portfolio Momentum exposure &lt;= 30%</p></li>
<li><p><code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">&quot;style</span> <span class="pre">&lt;=</span> <span class="pre">0.50&quot;</span></code> –&gt; sum of all style factor exposures (Momentum, Value, Size, etc.) &lt;= 50%</p></li>
</ul>
</div></blockquote>
<p>Factor constraints require a prior estimator (e.g.

<p>Asset, group, factor, and factor family names must be unique.</p>

<dt><strong>add_constraints</strong><span class="classifier">Callable[[cp.Variable], cp.Expression | list[cp.Expression]], optional</span></dt><dd><p>Add a custom constraint or a list of constraints to the existing constraints.

It must return a CVXPY expression or a list of CVXPY expressions, evaluated

<p>For example, to require an effective number of assets of at least 20:</p>
<div class="doctest highlight-default notranslate"><div class="highlight"><pre><span></span><span class="gp">&gt;&gt;&gt; </span><span class="kn">import</span><span class="w"> </span><span class="nn">cvxpy</span><span class="w"> </span><span class="k">as</span><span class="w"> </span><span class="nn">cp</span>
<span class="gp">&gt;&gt;&gt; </span><span class="kn">from</span><span class="w"> </span><span class="nn">skfolio.optimization</span><span class="w"> </span><span class="kn">import</span> <span class="n">MeanRisk</span>
<span class="gp">&gt;&gt;&gt; </span><span class="n">model</span> <span class="o">=</span> <span class="n">MeanRisk</span><span class="p">(</span><span class="n">add_constraints</span><span class="o">=</span><span class="k">lambda</span> <span class="n">w</span><span class="p">:</span> <span class="n">cp</span><span class="o">.</span><span class="n">sum_squares</span><span class="p">(</span><span class="n">w</span><span class="p">)</span> <span class="o">&lt;=</span> <span class="mi">1</span> <span class="o">/</span> <span class="mi">20</span><span class="p">)</span>
</pre></div>
</div>

including quantities estimated during <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">fit</span></code>. For example, to cap each
position size in risk units at 20 bps, using the volatilities estimated
by the prior:</p>
<div class="doctest highlight-default notranslate"><div class="highlight"><pre><span></span><span class="gp">&gt;&gt;&gt; </span><span class="kn">import</span><span class="w"> </span><span class="nn">numpy</span><span class="w"> </span><span class="k">as</span><span class="w"> </span><span class="nn">np</span>
<span class="gp">&gt;&gt;&gt; </span><span class="k">def</span><span class="w"> </span><span class="nf">position_risk_cap</span><span class="p">(</span><span class="n">w</span><span class="p">,</span> <span class="n">model</span><span class="p">):</span>
<span class="gp">... </span>    <span class="n">covariance</span> <span class="o">=</span> <span class="n">model</span><span class="o">.</span><span class="n">prior_estimator_</span><span class="o">.</span><span class="n">return_distribution_</span><span class="o">.</span><span class="n">covariance</span>
<span class="gp">... </span>    <span class="n">vols</span> <span class="o">=</span> <span class="n">np</span><span class="o">.</span><span class="n">sqrt</span><span class="p">(</span><span class="n">np</span><span class="o">.</span><span class="n">diag</span><span class="p">(</span><span class="n">covariance</span><span class="p">))</span>
<span class="gp">... </span>    <span class="k">return</span> <span class="n">cp</span><span class="o">.</span><span class="n">multiply</span><span class="p">(</span><span class="n">vols</span><span class="p">,</span> <span class="n">w</span><span class="p">)</span> <span class="o">&lt;=</span> <span class="mf">0.002</span>
<span class="gp">&gt;&gt;&gt; </span><span class="n">model</span> <span class="o">=</span> <span class="n">MeanRisk</span><span class="p">(</span><span class="n">add_constraints</span><span class="o">=</span><span class="n">position_risk_cap</span><span class="p">)</span>
</pre></div>
</div>
</dd>
<dt><strong>overwrite_expected_return</strong><span class="classifier">Callable[[cp.Variable], cp.Expression], optional</span></dt><dd><p>Overwrite the expected return <span class="math notranslate nohighlight">\(\mu \cdot w\)</span> with a custom CVXPY

<span class="gp">&gt;&gt;&gt; </span><span class="k">def</span><span class="w"> </span><span class="nf">geometric_expected_return</span><span class="p">(</span><span class="n">w</span><span class="p">,</span> <span class="n">model</span><span class="p">):</span>

<span class="gp">... </span>    <span class="k">return</span> <span class="n">dist</span><span class="o">.</span><span class="n">mu</span> <span class="o">@</span> <span class="n">w</span> <span class="o">-</span> <span class="mf">0.5</span> <span class="o">*</span> <span class="n">cp</span><span class="o">.</span><span class="n">quad_form</span><span class="p">(</span><span class="n">w</span><span class="p">,</span> <span class="n">dist</span><span class="o">.</span><span class="n">covariance</span><span class="p">)</span>
<span class="gp">&gt;&gt;&gt; </span><span class="n">model</span> <span class="o">=</span> <span class="n">MeanRisk</span><span class="p">(</span><span class="n">overwrite_expected_return</span><span class="o">=</span><span class="n">geometric_expected_return</span><span class="p">)</span>

(alone or in a list) to fall back to the estimator’s <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">previous_weights</span></code>.

estimator so that <code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">fit</span></code> still returns the original instance. For traceability,

</dd>

<code class="docutils literal notranslate"><span class="pre">FailedPortfolio</span></code>. When fallbacks are specified, this behavior applies only
after all fallbacks have been exhausted.

while updating stateful sub-estimators are always raised.</p>
