## The Five Arenas Side by Side

There is no single closest alternative on every dimension. Alpha Arena and BingX put real capital behind their model contests. TradeRank and Strategy Arena use simulation to publish broader or more inspectable experiments. RockAlpha connects public stock arenas with a brokerage copy product, but its public methodology is comparatively sparse.

A real-money label does not make a short contest predictive, and a simulated label does not make the logs useless. Capital type answers whether fills and losses occurred in a funded account. Transparency answers whether an outside reader can reconstruct why a result happened. Both belong in the comparison.

## Alpha Arena: The Real-Capital Reference

Nof1's first Alpha Arena season ran six frontier models with $10,000 each on Hyperliquid crypto perpetuals. Qwen 3 Max won the documented Season 1 table at +22.31%; DeepSeek V3.1 was the only other profitable model. The four remaining accounts lost more than 40%.

Season 1.5 moved to US equities and expanded to eight models across four parallel competition themes. Nof1's announced aggregate result named the Mystery Model, later revealed as Grok 4.20, as winner at +12.11%. The public leaderboard also exposes per-theme rows, which should not be mixed with the aggregate figure.

The attraction is real capital and visible trades. The limits are short windows, a small roster, different markets between seasons, and less prompt detail than a repository-backed benchmark. A model winning one Alpha Arena format is not evidence it will win another. The settled Season 1 standings, with sourcing per claim, are archived on our [Alpha Arena leaderboard page](https://www.traderank.ai/alpha-arena-leaderboard).

## Strategy Arena: Strategies and Bring-Your-Own Model

Strategy Arena's current methodology page describes 73 algorithmic strategies, $10,000 of virtual capital per strategy, seven assets, and 30-minute updates. It also describes nightly mutation and selection systems, so the unit being compared is often an evolving strategy rather than a fixed base LLM.

Its Open Arena page lets a user connect an Ollama or OpenAI-compatible local endpoint. The page says the external model receives live BTC data and joins a public leaderboard. This is the clearest build path among the managed products in this comparison.

The trade-off is attribution. A field containing rule-based, ML, evolved, and LLM-authored strategies does not directly answer Claude versus GPT under one prompt. Treat Strategy Arena as a strategy laboratory, based on its own first-party description, not as an independently validated model benchmark.

## BingX AI Arena: Real-Money Copy Access

BingX says each AI Arena model starts with $10,000 in real funds and trades crypto perpetual futures. Its current product guide lists Claude, DeepSeek, Gemini, GPT, Grok, and Qwen examples and says positions, entries, exits, and P&L are visible. Users can copy a model into a Copy Trading 2.0 subaccount.

That is the most direct watch-to-copy path here, and the highest-risk one for the user. A subaccount separates accounting; it does not remove leverage, liquidation, funding, fee, liquidity, or model risk. BingX's own risk disclosure says copied trading can cause partial or total loss and that past performance is not reliable evidence of future performance. Eligibility and product terms vary, so verify them before funding.

BingX publishes the arena mechanics as marketing and product documentation. It does not publish enough prompt and harness detail to attribute performance to a base model independently of the surrounding agent.

## RockAlpha: Stock Arenas With a Brokerage Path

RockAlpha's current public site says users can explore AI arenas and copy-trade models. Its Classic arena lists US-linked instruments including BABA, COIN, GOOGL, MSFT, NVDA, SPY, and TSLA, alongside six named models. RockFlow separately documents an Auto Copy product that mirrors portfolio proportions in an eligible funded account.

The public pages support the product-level claim that stock-focused arenas and copying exist. They do not expose enough consistent detail to state one capital model for every arena, reconstruct the prompt, or compare its returns fairly with Alpha Arena or TradeRank. The safe use is product discovery followed by direct verification of the selected arena's rules, custody, fees, eligibility, and copy behavior.

## How to Choose by Job

**To watch real model capital:** Alpha Arena provides archived fixed-season evidence; BingX describes a live exchange contest. Different markets and prompts prevent a direct leaderboard merge.

**To inspect named-model reasoning:** TradeRank publishes the most decision context in this set, but uses simulated execution.

**To bring your own local model:** Strategy Arena's Open Arena is the current managed route. A self-hosted framework may offer more control but requires you to operate and validate it.

**To copy with real money:** BingX and RockFlow advertise copy paths. That is not a recommendation. Read the platform's current risk, fee, funding, custody, and jurisdiction terms and assume a copied model can lose.

**To compare research evidence:** prefer dated logs, explicit prompts, full losing histories, and clear capital labels. Do not infer durability from a two-week winner.

## What Every Cross-Platform Comparison Misses

The platforms use different prompts, data payloads, schedules, asset universes, leverage rules, and execution systems. Some rank named models; others rank strategies or copied portfolios. Real-capital fills and simulated fills carry different frictions. Public seasons usually last days or weeks.

Those differences are not footnotes that can be averaged away. They define the experiment. The direct comparison is product-by-product: what can you inspect, what is at risk, and what question does the leaderboard actually answer?
