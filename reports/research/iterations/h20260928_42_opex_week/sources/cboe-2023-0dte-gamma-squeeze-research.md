## Abstract (summarized from the extracted text)
The large volume of trading in 0DTE (zero-days-to-expiration) S&P 500 index
options means options market makers (OMMs) can hold large, fast-changing
gamma positions. The paper estimates the maximum impact of OMM gamma on
index volatility using proprietary trade data, comparing realized
volatility to a counterfactual where OMM gamma has no effect, to isolate
the hedge-rebalancing channel's contribution to volatility. Keywords:
0DTE options, SPX options, S&P 500 index options, hedge rebalancing, gamma.

## Relevance to this iteration
Documents that the modern options market-microstructure landscape (large,
fast-growing 0DTE/short-dated options volume) differs structurally from the
market Stivers & Sun (2013) studied (1988-2010, before 0DTE options
existed) and from the earlier years of Garcia-Ares & Muravyev's own sample
(2005-2021). This is a caveat on whether either paper's mechanism transfers
unchanged to the 2024-2026 anchor window this iteration tests, not a
confirmation or refutation of the option-expiration-week stock-return
effect itself (this paper studies index volatility from 0DTE hedge
rebalancing generally, not the weekly calendar effect specifically).
