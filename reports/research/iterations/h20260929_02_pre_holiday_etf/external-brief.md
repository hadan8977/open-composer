# External Brief: h20260929_02_pre_holiday_etf

T-20260929-02 (dir:pre_holiday_effect_small_cap_tier). Ariel (1990) found US
market-index returns on the trading day before a holiday were 8.6-14.4x the
average non-pre-holiday return (1963-1982). Ko & Yang (2021, full text read
locally) extend the sample to 1983-2019 and find the premium now survives
only among small firms; the large-firm premium has essentially disappeared,
especially after 1990 (VW index pre/non-pre-holiday t-stat falls from 7.03
to 1.92, and DJIA/S&P 500 1983-2019 t-stats are 0.64/0.93, both
insignificant). Kim & Park (1994) and McLean & Pontiff (2016) are cited as
background (cross-market robustness historically, and general anomaly-decay
after publication as the likely mechanism for large-firm arbitrage-away).
An adjacent 2026 paper (Apergis) was found but blocked (403), not read, not
used. This iteration tests the claim at the ETF level: 4 small/micro-cap
ETFs (IWM, IJR, IWC, VB) vs 2 large-cap ETFs (SPY, QQQ), long only around
the last trading day before a federal-market-holiday closure, hold 1 or 2
sessions, else cash (SHY), plus 6 per-ticker frequency-matched random-day
placebos as an internal falsification control. See `direction-review.json`
and `external-brief.json` for the full source list, reflections and the
2026-09-29 02:1x UTC coordinator revision that replaced the originally
planned signal_card event-mode approach with this direct calendar-rule
batch.
