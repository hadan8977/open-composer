In last week’s weekly open, TPTP flagged a clean breakout on XLV that has been met with continuation since our last update. Healthcare continues to show relative strength while SPY pauses, driven by pullbacks in technology stocks.
Financials profiting from rotational flows after weak performance YTD

Industrials have been leading, and keep leading

Weekly Open #1: BTC Hanging On A Thread
Jun 22
Read full story
It’s been a while since we provided an update on our BTC Strategies, as they haven’t taken a position in a long time. I would like to point out that this is a good thing. These 2 strategies are designed to be long during bull markets, capturing explosive moves while protecting capital during bear markets. 
BTC 1D Gaussian Channel Strategy
BTC is well below its 200 SMA + in a daily downtrend. This market phase is exactly why I have allocated some of my personal holdings towards these trending engines. They protect capital, the drawdown becomes manageable. 
The Strategy has not entered a position since May 21st. It took a small loss on the rally attempt of BTC, finding resistance at 82,500 USD (200 SMA).
Currently, there is no threat of entering a position in the near future. The upper band of the Gaussian channel is located at 73,811 USD. 
5D 5EMA
In contrast to our Gaussian Channel setup, this strategy did a better job at capturing the latest move from 66,000 USD to 81,000 USD, making a decent gain along the way:
The strategy might open a long earlier than the Gaussian Channel Strategy, at a 5D closure above 64,930 USD. This is a function of the speed at which both filters (5D 5EMA vs. 1D Gaussian Channel) adjust to recent price action, which makes them very complementary. One Strategy goes in earlier, at the risk of getting chopped around a lot, while the other one waits longer for confirmation, catching less of the actual move but with a higher overall win rate.

Bonds
The easy money has been made
Last week, we flagged that 10-year Bund Yields beautifully retraced into a major support area around the 55EMA on the weekly, or their 200SMA on the daily. Since then, they have launched higher. 
The Bond market has been better at pricing the benign risk of inflation at this point in the cycle, retracing simultaneously with Crude Oil since May. To be clear: I do think the easy money has been made in this trade. Now it is time to look for the long-term drivers of bund yields (no growth, rising debt, aging population, productivity slump, etc.) that will take over and continue the long-term trend. 
The structural case for higher Bund yields hasn't changed, it was simply temporarily overshadowed by falling oil prices. With that tailwind fading, I assume the long-term drivers will take over from here.
