 Improving the Opening Range Breakout on SPY Subscribe Sign in Articles Improving the Opening Range Breakout Strategy on SPY Simple, Economically Motivated Enhancements to a Classic Intraday Trading Strategy Concretum Research Jul 08, 2026 ∙ Paid 8 Share The persistence of Opening Range Breakout (ORB) strategies as a viable source of compelling risk-adjusted returns remains an open debate. In this article, we conduct a comprehensive empirical analysis of a classic 5-minute ORB framework applied to SPY over the period January 2008 to December 2025. Our results indicate that, in its plain-vanilla form , the strategy fails to generate meaningful net-of-fee returns : commissions and market impact erode the gross edge almost entirely, and participation on virtually every trading session leaves the strategy fully exposed to cumulative trading costs. Rather than abandoning the ORB concept altogether, however, we ask a different question: Can a handful of simple, economically motivated improvements transform an otherwise mediocre strategy into an attractive systematic trading framework? Specifically, we investigate whether a combination of intuitive trade-selection filters , improved trade management and dynamic position - sizing techniques can materially improve both returns and risk-adjusted performance without altering the core ORB philosophy or relying on extensive parameter optimization. Our findings suggest that the answer is yes! As illustrated in Figure 1, these enhancements increase the strategy's CAGR from 1.0% to 12.1% , improve its Sharpe ratio from 0.14 to 0.84 , and raise annualized alpha from an economically modest and statistically insignificant 1.75% to a highly significant 12.80%, all while remaining robust under conservative assumptions for commissions and market impact. The edge lies not in inventing a new ORB signal, but in learning how to trade the existing one more intelligently. In the remainder of this article, we explain each enhancement individually, discuss the economic intuition behind it, and quantify its incremental contribution to the strategy's overall performance.
