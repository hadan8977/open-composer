      About

      Daily, rules‑based Nasdaq‑100 strategy: uses 3× long (TQQQ) in uptrends, steps to T‑bills when momentum weak or overheated, and may use 50% −3× (SQQQ) in downtrends. Signals come from moving averages and RSI to size and time risk.
      How it works
      Trades: QQQ (Nasdaq‑100, big tech), TQQQ (3× QQQ), SQQQ (−3× QQQ), and a T‑bill fund (cash‑like). If QQQ > 200‑day avg, it holds TQQQ (100% if strong; 70%/30% with T‑bills if softer), buys dips (RSI<30), and shifts to T‑bills if overheated (RSI>78) or <50‑day avg. If QQQ < 200‑day avg, it holds T‑bills; may use 50% SQQQ when falling, or 25% TQQQ when steadier. RSI=0–100 “heat” gauge.
      Value prop
      Tap Nasdaq-100 momentum with a disciplined, rule-based strategy: levered uptrends, cash hedges in risk-off moments. OOS: 36.4% annualized return, Calmar 1.21—offering meaningful upside with measured risk relative to a plain S&P exposure.

      A Composer symphony is an automated trading strategy that executes trades based on parameters of your choice. Some symphonies are similar to holding one ETF in normal conditions and rotating to a different ETF when market conditions shift, for example a 5% drop in the S&P 500, while others use complex rules with dozens of triggers. However, complex doesn’t always mean better. A simple, well-structured symphony can be just as effective as an intricate one. Learn more about how symphonies work here.
