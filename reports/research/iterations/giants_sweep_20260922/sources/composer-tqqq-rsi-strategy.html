      About

      A daily, tactical “buy‑the‑dip in uptrends” strategy using leveraged tech/Nasdaq ETFs. It owns TQQQ when the long‑term trend is up and momentum is weak, parks in SHV when not, and may briefly flip to SQQQ/UVXY after extreme surges or buy TECL on deep sell‑offs.
      How it works
      Daily, it rotates among a few ETFs based on price signals: - Trend filter: only buy during uptrends (price above its 200‑day average). - “Speed” checks: RSI (0–100; low=oversold, high=overheated) and PPO (short vs long averages; below 0=weak). - Core: buy TQQQ (3x Nasdaq‑100) on dips; park in SHV (short‑term Treasuries) otherwise. - After extreme up days/overheating, briefly use SQQQ (−3x Nasdaq‑100) or UVXY (VIX/fear). - Sometimes buy TECL (3x Tech) on deep sell‑offs.
      Value prop
      Out-of-sample, this tactical tech/Nasdaq strategy delivers ~41% annualized return vs ~22% for the S&P, with solid risk control (Calmar ~1.24) and disciplined hedges (SHV, SQQQ/UVXY). Higher upside, but higher drawdown than the S&P.

      A Composer symphony is an automated trading strategy that executes trades based on parameters of your choice. Some symphonies are similar to holding one ETF in normal conditions and rotating to a different ETF when market conditions shift, for example a 5% drop in the S&P 500, while others use complex rules with dozens of triggers. However, complex doesn’t always mean better. A simple, well-structured symphony can be just as effective as an intricate one. Learn more about how symphonies work here.
