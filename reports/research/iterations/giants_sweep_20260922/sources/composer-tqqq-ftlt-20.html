      About

      High‑risk, tech‑heavy rotation. Above the 200‑day trend it mostly rides the Nasdaq with leverage but jumps into volatility funds when markets look overheated. Below trend it buys oversold bounces or flips to short tech or Treasuries.
      How it works
      Trend: if SPY > 200‑day avg = risk‑on; else risk‑off. RSI = 0–100 heat of recent moves (high=overbought, low=oversold). Risk‑on: if tech is very hot, rotate into volatility (UVXY/VIXY) unless they just crashed; otherwise ride Nasdaq with leverage (prefer TQQQ; if weaker, QQQ/QLD), only flipping to VIXY on fear spikes. Risk‑off: buy brief rebounds (TECL/UPRO) when deeply oversold; else hold SQQQ (short tech) or TLT (Treasuries), whichever is stronger. Tickers: QQQ=Nasdaq‑100; TQQQ 3x; QLD 2x; SQQQ −3x; SPXL/UPRO 3x S&P; TECL 3x; VIXY/UVXY=volatility; TLT=bonds.
      Value prop
      Targeting ~29.7% annualized return vs 20% for the S&P in out-of-sample history, with tactical tech leadership, leverage, and volatility hedges. Higher upside, but larger drawdowns—fits growth-focused investors who tolerate volatility.

      A Composer symphony is an automated trading strategy that executes trades based on parameters of your choice. Some symphonies are similar to holding one ETF in normal conditions and rotating to a different ETF when market conditions shift, for example a 5% drop in the S&P 500, while others use complex rules with dozens of triggers. However, complex doesn’t always mean better. A simple, well-structured symphony can be just as effective as an intricate one. Learn more about how symphonies work here.
