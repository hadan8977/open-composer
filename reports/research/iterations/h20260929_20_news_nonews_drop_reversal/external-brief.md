# External Brief: h20260929_20_news_nonews_drop_reversal

See `external-brief.json` for the full 8-source table (3 papers, 2 cost-
plausibility posts/platform docs, 2 internal design notes, plus this
project's own PIT data coverage). Summary: Chan (2003, JFE) and Savor (2012,
JFE) independently document that large individual-stock price moves without
identifiable public news tend to reverse, while moves with bad news drift
further -- both concentrated in smaller/less liquid names. Dyl, Yuksel &
Zaynutdinova (2019, JBR) independently replicate the pattern through 2015 and
document that the effect's magnitude shrank as market liquidity improved
post-decimalization; no post-2020 independent replication was found in this
session's search. Borrow-fee and spread-cost sources (Interactive Brokers,
QuantRocket, a liquidity/spread explainer) support the plausibility of this
iteration's preregistered cost assumptions (1%/year borrow fee on the short
leg, ADV-tiered spread costs) without altering them.
