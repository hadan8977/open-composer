# Savor (2012), "Stock returns after major price shocks: The impact of information", Journal of Financial Economics 106(3), pp. 635-659

Fetched: 2026-09-29 via WebSearch (Claude built-in); primary listing `https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X12001377` and Wharton faculty PDF page `https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/stock-returns-after-major-price-shocks-the-impact-of-information-publishedpaper-file/` (abstract/summary level only; full PDF not fetched this pass).

## Claim (abstract/summary level)

Uses analyst-report activity as a proxy for "information presence" around major one-day price shocks:

- Price shocks **accompanied by information** (analyst reports) are followed by **drift** (continuation).
- Price shocks **without information** are followed by **reversal**.
- Interpretation: investors **underreact** to news about fundamentals and **overreact** to price shocks that are not fundamentally justified.
- Information-based price changes correlate more strongly with future earnings surprises than no-information ones.
- Drift is present only when the direction of the price move and the change in analyst recommendations have the same sign.

## Relationship to this iteration

Same mechanism family as Chan (2003), independent identification method (analyst reports vs. headline database) reaching the same qualitative conclusion — this is the second, methodologically distinct piece of evidence behind the brief's design, not a mechanical duplicate.
