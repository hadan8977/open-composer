Understanding managed futures

Managed futures strategies seek to identify sustained price trends across equities, fixed income, currencies and commodities. They can take long positions in rising markets and short positions in falling markets, typically using liquid futures contracts.

The principle is straightforward: follow price trends rather than rely on forecasts about where markets should go. This flexibility allows managed futures to pursue opportunities across different markets and in either direction.

Managed futures can provide a return source that behaves differently from stocks and bonds. Because they can invest across multiple markets and take both long and short positions, their returns are not dependent on any single asset class rising.

Their historically low correlation with traditional investments can help broaden portfolio diversification and may offer resilience during periods of sustained market stress. Diversification does not guarantee a profit or protect against loss.

Understanding DBMF

Many managed futures funds rely on a single manager and a single set of models. DBMF takes a different approach.

It seeks to replicate the core positioning of the 20 leading managed futures hedge funds represented in the SG CTA Index. Its model analyses the index’s daily returns and expresses the resulting exposures through a focused portfolio of highly liquid futures contracts.

By drawing on a diversified group of managers, DBMF aims to reduce reliance on any one investment approach.

DBMF provides access to managed futures through a liquid, rules-based ETF.

Rather than relying on a single manager, it seeks to capture the core positioning of 20 leading managed futures hedge funds. These exposures are implemented through highly liquid futures contracts and rebalanced weekly.

The aim is to capture the principal return drivers of the category while reducing manager-selection risk, fees and implementation complexity.

DBMF combines elements of both.

The fund uses a rules-based model to estimate the positioning of an index composed of 20 actively managed futures hedge funds. It then implements those exposures through liquid futures contracts.

DBMF does not make discretionary market forecasts. Instead, its model responds systematically to changes in the index’s underlying positioning.

Portfolio considerations

DBMF is designed to complement a traditional portfolio by adding a potential return source that is less dependent on the direction of stock and bond markets.

Depending on an investor’s objectives, an allocation could be funded from equities, fixed income, cash or an existing alternatives allocation. The appropriate approach will depend on the role managed futures are expected to play within the wider portfolio.

There is no single allocation that is right for every investor. The appropriate size will depend on factors including investment objectives, risk tolerance, time horizon and the composition of the existing portfolio.

An allocation should be meaningful enough to contribute when managed futures perform well, while remaining appropriate during weaker periods. For some balanced portfolios, a 5% to 10% allocation may provide a practical starting point for discussion, although individual circumstances will vary.

Investors should consider any allocation decision with a financial professional.

DBMF earns interest on the collateral held within the portfolio, typically in short-term US Treasury bills. This income is generally distributed quarterly, while capital gains are generally distributed annually.

Distributions are not guaranteed and may vary. Investors should refer to the latest fund documents for current distribution information.
