# External brief: prediction-market odds and ETF returns (searched 2026-10-07, sources in `reports/research/intel/sources/pm-event-study-2026-10-07/`)

## How others do it
1. **Diercks, Katz, Wright (Fed Board FEDS 2026-010, "Kalshi and the Rise of Macro Markets")** compare Kalshi forecasts with fed funds futures, Bloomberg consensus and surveys. Kalshi's fed-funds mode was right the day before every FOMC since 2022; headline CPI beats Bloomberg consensus; core CPI and unemployment are about equal. They document intraday jumps of Kalshi probabilities after releases but do not trade ETFs. Implication: pre-release Kalshi information beyond consensus exists only for headline CPI and the policy rate, so a pre-release lean in other classes should carry little.
2. **Li and Xie (Keio WP, 2026-04, "Trade Policy Surprises and U.S. Convenience Yields")** build a high-frequency trade-policy surprise from Polymarket tariff contracts: change of the last trade before to the first trade after a 30-minute window [t-10, t+20] around each announcement; a one-standard-deviation surprise lowers the S&P 500 about 0.7 percent initially and persistently, Treasury convenience yields fall. Implication: tariff odds jumps are priced by equities within the window; the open question is whether anything is left after the window, which is what the 15-240 minute horizons test. They use transaction data; we use hourly prices, so our jump timing is coarser.
3. **Tsang and Yang (arXiv 2603.03152, 2603.03136, 2026)**: in 2024 election markets the debate jump largely reversed, the assassination-attempt jump persisted, Biden's dropout moved nothing: price adjustment depends on how anticipated the news was and on linked outcomes. Implication: continuation versus reversal cannot be assumed, so both signs are counted as trials.
4. **Mohanty and Krishnamachari (arXiv 2604.01431, 2026-04)**: daily Kalshi macro probability changes predict crypto volatility (KXFED t 3.6 in sample, out-of-sample gains only for recession and CPI channels; reversal after the cutting cycle ended). Implication: volatility, not direction; effects are regime dependent; they correct for multiple tests (Benjamini-Hochberg).
5. **Local priors**: h20261002_pm_macro_uncertainty (uncertainty vs daily premium: not supported), h20261004_pm_midterm_odds_leadlag (Sharpe -1.2, daily), h20261004_pm_earnings_beat_odds (refuted). Nothing local covers minutes-to-hours ETF reaction.
6. **Platform facts**: Polymarket CLOB prices-history needs startTs/endTs windows of at most about 14 days for hourly fidelity (probed 2026-10-07: 15 days OK, 30 days rejected) and honours fidelity in minutes; Kalshi candlesticks are 1, 60 or 1440 minutes, historical endpoint for settled markets.

## Which we follow, what changes
- Follow Li-Xie's identification idea (odds change in a short window around news, ETF return right after) for J, and Diercks et al.'s Kalshi release classes for R.
- Changes: (a) no outside consensus data (Bloomberg is not free), so R uses Kalshi's own drift into the release and its gap to the previous print; (b) hourly Polymarket points, not trades, so J detects one-hour jumps; (c) direction mapping by theme fixed before looking at returns; (d) trade the ETF net of extended-hours spreads, with timing placebos and a realised-surprise positive control, which none of the papers has; (e) counts of trials and DSR across both parts.
- Expected result by the literature: R weak (Kalshi is about consensus), J small at best (equities react inside the odds window). A negative result at depth is a useful answer.
