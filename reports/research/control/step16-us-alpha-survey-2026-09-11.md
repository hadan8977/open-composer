## Ranked shortlist -- what to build first, and why

1. Overnight-vs-intraday timing gate (item 4): strongest verified numbers in this survey (3.47%/-3.02% monthly alphas, t-stats in the teens), costs nothing extra since it only needs daily open/close already in hand.
2. Short interest days-to-cover screen column (item 2): single verified number (1.2%/month long-short) from a clean, free, biweekly FINRA feed with no engineering complexity.
3. Intraday first/last-half-hour momentum on SPY/QQQ (item 5): verified OOS R² up to 2.0% (6.9% in crisis-like high-vol regimes), and it is the one direction that maps directly onto the program's own minute-bar-momentum phase.
4. Chen-Zimmermann open-source asset-pricing pull (item 10, unresearched here): free, direct, current-sample returns for ~200+ anomalies -- do this fetch before building anything else, since it can validate or kill several of the other rows cheaply.
5. Kyle-lambda / signed-order-flow tilt from daily OHLCV (item 7): free and fresh (Jul 2026 preprint) but the paper's own R²~0 result means it belongs as a minor secondary tilt column, not a headline strategy.
6. Opportunistic-insider screen column (item 1): real original effect, but budget for the 2024-era decayed magnitude (~0.3-0.4%/month), not the 2012 number, when sizing any promotion gate.
7. 13F conviction/consensus quarterly tilt (item 3): verified Sharpe 0.75 / 3.80% outperformance, but stale sample (ends 2019) and a 45-day disclosure lag cap how much weight this should carry.
8. LLM EDGAR/earnings-call sentiment packet (item 9): promising headline numbers (90% hit rate, drift prediction) but unverified this round -- re-fetch Lopez-Lira/Tang and Kim-Muhn-Nikolaev directly before spending engineering time.
9. Gap fade / vol-compression breakout (item 6): no literature support found at all -- only build if treated as a from-scratch in-house hypothesis test with full backtest-forensics review, never cite the blog numbers.
10. Retail attention from Reddit/WSB or Google Trends (item 8): lowest priority -- evidence is contradictory (contrarian vs. momentum framings) and Reddit's API is no longer reliably free, so the data-access problem now dominates the alpha question.

**Does this change the ranked shortlist?** Only at the margins, not a reordering of the top of the
list:

- **Item 10 stays a prerequisite, not a promoted headline item.** It was already ranked #4 ("do this
  fetch before building anything else") precisely because it is a benchmarking/context tool rather
  than a standalone strategy, and that is confirmed correct. Its content is more consequential than
  its rank position suggests, though: the direct quote **"these results do not account for trading
  costs... Chen and Velikov (2019) find that the remaining predictability is eliminated by effective
  bid-ask spreads"** is a severe caveat sitting right next to the reassuring "decay does not wipe out
  the in-sample return" headline. Net effect: every item in this survey's shortlist (not just item
  10) should now be treated as gross-of-cost and pre-decay unless explicitly re-tested net-of-cost
  in-repo -- this is a cross-cutting update to how *all* eleven rows should be read, more than a
  reason to move any single row up or down.
- **Item 9 modestly strengthens, and specifically strengthens, the case for keeping W4b's
  preregistration gate mandatory.** `docs/plan-post-reset-roadmap-2026-09-11.zh.md`'s W4b row
  describes the LLM factor-generator loop as "LLM proposes formulas (restricted operator DSL) ->
  screener evaluation -> experience memory -> iterate; preregistration pass criteria." This
  continuation's single biggest finding for item 9 is that Kim-Muhn-Nikolaev's "Bloated Disclosures"
  -- a paper cited uncritically as search-synthesis in round 1 -- turns out to be **withdrawn by its
  own authors after a failed internal replication**. That is close to a worked example of exactly the
  failure mode W4b's screener-evaluation and preregistration gate exists to catch before a factor
  reaches paper trading. Lopez-Lira/Tang's surviving result is real (confirmed by direct fetch) but
  is only a qualitative hit-rate/direction claim that the same abstract says is *itself decaying* as
  LLM adoption spreads -- not strong enough on its own to promote item 9 above items 2/4/5/7 in the
  build order. Recommendation: no change to build order, but treat this as a concrete argument
  against ever relaxing W4b's gate for "obviously good" LLM-sourced candidates.
- **Item 6 gets a new, narrower candidate rather than a promotion.** The verified "End-of-Day
  Reversal" numbers (3.78-6.38 bps/day, t-stats 10-17) are stronger than anything else found for a
  2023-2026-dated result in this entire survey, round 1 included -- but the signal needs intraday
  bars sampled at a fixed clock time, not free daily OHLCV, so it belongs conceptually next to item 5
  (intraday momentum, same data tier) rather than remaining grouped with the literal gap-fade/
  vol-compression hypotheses, which are still zero-evidence and still should not be built from
  literature support.
- **Items 8 and 11 are unchanged in priority.** Item 8's direction is now much better documented
  (short-horizon momentum that reverses; WSB predictability specifically decayed after the January
  2021 GameStop squeeze) but still has no confirmed effect-size magnitude and the same Reddit-API
  data-access constraint, so it stays low priority. Item 11 remains a confirmed open question with no
  external benchmark to chase, unchanged from round 1's conclusion.
