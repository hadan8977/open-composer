# Contextual UI pattern catalog

Use with [avoid-ai-design](../SKILL.md). These are diagnostic prompts, not authorship evidence or automatic violations. Severity comes from the observed effect on the task or design goal.

A code match establishes presence. Hierarchy, rhythm, identity, visual weight, and readability need a render. The selected direction can legitimately use any of the techniques below.

## Typography

**Observe:** no meaningful difference between titles, labels, supporting text, and body; decorative type obscures content; every screen repeats a heading treatment unrelated to the content.

**Check:** scale, weight, line height, measure, wrapping, language coverage, and audience fit. One neutral font can support a complete hierarchy. A second font is not required.

**Improve:** adjust roles and relationships within the existing system. Change the typeface when the brief or observed legibility/identity problem justifies it, not because the name appears on a list.

## Color and material

**Observe:** too many competing accents, state colors confused with decoration, unreadable text over effects, or repeated glass/shadows making every surface appear equally important.

**Check:** color roles, focal hierarchy, composite contrast, and the actual material brief. Blue, purple, gradients, monochrome, and neutral gray are all legitimate choices.

**Improve:** clarify roles, reduce redundant emphasis, or tune the selected material. Use liquid-materials for a specific rendering problem rather than banning glass across the product.

## Layout and composition

**Observe:** a template dictates section order, all content is forced into identical feature cards, important actions are buried, or large gaps separate items that belong together.

**Check:** content relationships and task order. Uniform rows/cards can make comparable items easier to scan. A centered hero or a conventional sidebar can fit the task.

**Improve:** regroup content, change relative scale, establish alignment, or vary composition where information warrants it. Do not replace predictable task UI with novelty for its own sake.

## Controls and chrome

**Observe:** decorative pills, icon tiles, badges, frames, or arrows repeat without conveying selection, category, action, or status.

**Check:** whether the element adds meaning, wayfinding, useful affordance, or product identity. Functional counts and category labels should remain.

**Improve:** remove or soften only redundant treatment, retaining accessible names, control geometry, focus, and touch access. The assembled screen decides whether an isolated component is too heavy.

## Motion and feedback

**Observe:** identical entrances across unrelated sections, continuous decorative activity, layout shifts, or completion feedback disconnected from real work.

**Check:** whether motion communicates state or supports the chosen concept, and whether reduced-motion and interruption behavior remain usable.

**Improve:** connect feedback to real operations, stabilize layout, and tune or remove distracting animation. Static interfaces do not need motion added just to appear designed.

## Copy and evidence

**Observe:** interchangeable slogans, unsupported metrics or customer claims, labels that conceal the action, or explanations compensating for confusing layout.

**Check:** actual product truth and the reader's task. Preserve necessary technical language and deliberate voice.

**Improve:** use specific supplied facts and clear action labels; omit unsupported claims or label illustrative material. Use no-ai-slop for a substantive prose edit.

## Functional omissions

Dead controls, inaccessible focus, unrecoverable errors, clipped content, and missing relevant states are concrete defects. Inspect or exercise the affected path. Prioritize these over stylistic preferences and distinguish the fix from a full redesign.
