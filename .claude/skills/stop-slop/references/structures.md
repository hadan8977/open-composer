# Structure candidates

Judge a structure by its job in the passage.

- **Repeated setup:** remove frames that announce a point without helping the reader understand it.
- **Contrast:** keep a real distinction or correction; simplify a repeated “not X, but Y” template when X is irrelevant.
- **Questions:** keep genuine questions, useful headings, and deliberate voice. Remove repetitive self-answered hooks that delay an obvious statement.
- **Fragments:** keep intentional emphasis and spoken cadence. Combine fragments only when their accumulation becomes distracting or unclear.
- **Lists:** use the number of items the content needs. Do not force either two or three.
- **Agency:** name a known responsible actor when it matters. Keep passive constructions and inanimate subjects when they accurately express cause, state, or focus.
- **Rhythm and punctuation:** avoid monotony without applying numerical quotas. Dashes, colons, and parentheses are tools, not authorship evidence.
- **Endings:** keep a useful conclusion or next action. Remove a recap or aphorism that adds nothing for this format.

Do not reorganize a whole argument under a request for a light wording cleanup. Preserve facts and uncertainty through every structural change.
