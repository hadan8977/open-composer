# Faithful cleanup examples

These examples demonstrate local edits, not rules to apply mechanically.

## Empty setup

Before: “It is important to note that the preview expires after 24 hours.”

After: “The preview expires after 24 hours.”

The time limit is preserved.

## Inflated wording

Before: “You can utilize the filter to locate invoices from March.”

After: “Use the filter to find invoices from March.”

No new capability or outcome is claimed.

## Preserve the limit

Before: “In most cases, the upload usually finishes within a minute.”

After: “The upload usually finishes within a minute.”

The repeated qualifier is removed; the sentence still does not promise every upload will finish within a minute.

## Preserve accurate behavior

Original: “The token expires automatically after 30 minutes.”

Keep it. The inanimate subject and adverb describe the behavior precisely.

## Preserve a meaningful contrast

Original: “Deleting the shortcut does not delete the original file.”

Keep it. The negative statement answers a real user concern.

## Missing evidence

Original: “Our tool transforms team productivity.”

Flag the unsupported scale of the claim when reviewing. If no supporting facts are available, do not invent percentages, saved hours, customers, or testimonials to make it sound concrete.
