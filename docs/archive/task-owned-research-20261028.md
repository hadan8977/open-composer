## Principles for changing the method

1. **Optimize for economic evidence.** Reuse public work, data and implementations. Before costly design choices, and when consequential research or technical problems arise, use targeted primary-source and implementation research instead of inventing every solution; reuse fresh evidence for routine repairs. A priority adjustment continues the existing plan unless the owner requests a new selection exercise. A process change should improve decisions, turnaround or an actual operational problem; code, tests and reports are not outcome measures.
2. **Give the team judgment.** The complete owner and independent peer handle ordinary science, experiments, implementation, optimization and repairs. At a substantive route outcome, the main session owns the technical continuation/pivot and next assignment. Only owner-level goals, risk, major priorities, cross-task conflicts or actual authority/spending need owner escalation. When the owner is unavailable, act within standing authority.
3. **Keep useful constraints.** Validate risks that affect this claim or current operations. Do not require later deployment evidence for an earlier method study, or approve every repair separately. Replace old self-imposed technical gates through a valid prospective delegation; never forge historical approval.
4. **Let methods evolve.** Owners may remove, merge or replace self-imposed workflows, skills, tools and roles as capability improves. Model brands, agent counts and test/review counts are not permanent rules. Evaluate useful results and turnaround without creating another approval platform.
5. **Preserve facts and authority.** Retain source evidence, failures, seen-data exposure and cumulative usage. Do not retrospectively change frozen success criteria. Correct mistaken summaries with provenance. Spending, devices, orders and production remain subject to actual authorization.
